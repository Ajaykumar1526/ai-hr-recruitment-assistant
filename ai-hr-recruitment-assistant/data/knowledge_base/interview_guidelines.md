# Interview Guidelines

## General Principles
- Structure every interview around the job's core requirements, not the candidate's resume alone.
- Ask open-ended questions that require the candidate to explain their reasoning, not just recall facts.
- Use the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to evaluate behavioral answers.
- Give every candidate the same core question set for a given role, so answers can be compared fairly.
- Never ask about age, marital status, religion, disability, pregnancy, or family plans.

## Technical Interviews
- Start with a warm-up question the candidate can answer confidently.
- Ask candidates to walk through a real project from their resume in technical depth.
- For missing/partial-match skills, ask a foundational question to gauge how quickly they could ramp up.
- Include at least one applied/practical question (e.g., "how would you approach X problem"), not just definitions.

## Behavioral Interviews
- Focus on collaboration, ownership, handling ambiguity, and learning from failure.
- Ask for specific examples, not hypotheticals, when possible.
- Probe for what the candidate personally did versus what the team did.

## Evaluation
- Score each answer against a specific evaluation point, not general impression.
- Separate "can do the job" (skills/experience) from "will do the job well" (motivation/fit).
- Document evidence for each rating so decisions can be reviewed and justified.
